# Government & Administration

## Why this segment exists
Brief me on what the U.S. federal government and the current
administration actually did today. I want concrete actions —
executive orders, agency rule-makings, congressional bills moving,
court rulings, regulatory enforcement, foreign-policy moves. Not punditry
or polling, not "Republicans vs. Democrats" framing, not horse-race
political coverage.

## What to cover
Cover anything that meets the bar of "a thing actually happened today."

### White House and executive branch
- Executive orders signed (with the actual policy mechanism, not just headline)
- Presidential statements that signal a real policy shift
- Cabinet appointments, confirmations, departures
- Agency leadership changes (Fed, FCC, FTC, SEC, EPA, etc.)

### Federal agencies (concrete actions only)
- New rules or rule-makings published in the Federal Register
- Enforcement actions (DOJ indictments, SEC charges, FTC complaints)
- Major regulatory decisions (FDA approvals, FCC orders, EPA standards)
- Major contract awards (DoD, GSA, etc.) when newsworthy

### Congress
- Bills that passed a chamber or got out of committee
- Funding deals, appropriations, debt-ceiling progress
- Notable hearings with substantive testimony (not theater)

### Courts
- Supreme Court rulings, opinions, cert grants/denials
- Major federal circuit rulings on tech, antitrust, AI, immigration,
  administrative law
- DOJ prosecutorial moves on big cases

### Foreign policy and national security
- Sanctions changes, new designations, OFAC actions
- Tariff announcements with effective dates
- Treaty / agreement signings or withdrawals
- Major DoD announcements, AUKUS / NATO developments
- State Department briefings with substantive news

### Tech, AI, and chip policy specifically
- Export controls (BIS rules, entity-list changes)
- AI executive orders, NIST AI guidance, OMB AI memos
- CHIPS Act disbursements, fab construction milestones
- Section 230 / online-speech regulation
- Antitrust against megacap tech

## Sources to prioritize
**Primary government sources** (always check first — these are the
"a thing actually happened" feed):
- whitehouse.gov briefing room and statements
- Federal Register (federalregister.gov) for new rules
- congress.gov for bill status and floor actions
- supremecourt.gov for opinions and orders
- BIS.doc.gov for export-control updates
- treasury.gov / OFAC for sanctions
- fcc.gov, ftc.gov, sec.gov, fda.gov for agency-specific actions

**Wire services and serious press** (for coverage and context):
- AP, Reuters, Bloomberg Government
- The Wall Street Journal, The New York Times (politics desk)
- Politico, Axios (good for "what just happened" news, skim the
  punditry)
- Lawfare, Just Security for legal/national-security analysis
- Tech-policy specific: Protocol, The Information, Tech Policy Press

## What to skip
- Polling data, political horse-race coverage, "could this hurt the
  midterms" framing
- Pundit takes and op-eds (unless an op-ed IS the news, like a major
  cabinet member resigning via Op-Ed)
- Twitter/X feuds between politicians
- Speculative "sources say" pieces with no concrete action attached
- State-level news unless it's a major bellwether (Texas, California,
  Florida, NY) AND has direct national implications

## Format / tone
- Length: ~3 minutes spoken
- Style: narrator-led, factual, news-wire register. Sam/Jordan can
  break in on a single big story (e.g., a major Supreme Court
  ruling) but most days the narrator carries it.
- Tone: dry, "just the facts." If the agent finds itself wanting to
  characterize motives, stop and report what was done instead.

## Recurring threads to watch
- AI / chip export-control regime (semiannual rule cycle)
- Antitrust suits against Google, Apple, Amazon, Meta
- CHIPS Act fab buildouts (TSMC AZ, Intel OH, Samsung TX, Micron NY)
- Federal AI policy (NIST, OMB, executive orders)
- Major Supreme Court terms (admin law, tech, speech)
