# Cold Open + Show Intro

## Why this segment exists
This is the cold open and the show's intro. It's the first thing the
listener hears. It must hook them in the first 10 seconds, then settle
into a quick "this is the show, this is what's coming" beat.

## Structure
1. **Cold open (≈10-15 seconds).** Emily teases the single most
   interesting thread of today's rundown in one or two sentences —
   it should make me want to keep listening. Aaron reacts ("wait, what?"
   / "we'll get there in a second" / "okay, hold that thought") and
   transitions into the formal intro.
2. **Show open (≈20 seconds).** Aaron greets the listener, names the
   show, says today's date, and previews the rundown in one breath —
   ("Today we've got AI news, the markets, what's happening in
   Washington, frontier tech, video games, and the rapid round at the
   end."). Then he hands off to Emily to start the first real segment.

## Sourcing for the cold-open tease
Emily's tease should pull from the most striking item across the day's
research briefs (Aaron sees the rundown when assembling the open).
Prioritize, in order:
- A genuine "wait, that's wild" technology or science breakthrough
- A market move that materially affected my positions
- A government action with real consequence
- A game I'd want to play

Skip cold-open teases that are ambiguous, hedged, or rumor-stage. The
tease should be concrete enough to land in 15 seconds.

## Format / tone
- Length: ~30-45 seconds spoken total
- Style: two-host conversational
- Tone: warm, low-key, fast — a friend telling you what they heard.
  Don't over-produce; don't sound like FM radio. The cold open should
  feel like a real reaction, not a pitch.

## Skip research
This segment doesn't need its own research call — the cold-open content
comes from the other segments' research briefs, which the script writer
sees when composing the show.
